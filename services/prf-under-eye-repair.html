What it is

How PRF under eye repair works

PRF is a concentrate prepared from a small sample of your blood. The platelets, white blood cells, and fibrin in the concentrate carry your body's own healing components to the treatment area.

Katherine explains how PRF under-eye repair works, how the treatment is placed around the delicate eye area, and whether it fits your goals.

Who it helps
Adults who want the area under their eyes to look refreshed.
Your visit
A consultation, a small blood draw, and placement in the treatment area.
Results
Results develop gradually over several weeks as your skin responds.
Aftercare
Written instructions, reviewed in person and emailed after your appointment.

How it works

Three clear steps

Katherine explains each step before it happens, so you know what to expect.

1

Blood sample

A small amount of blood is drawn, the same way as a routine lab test.

2

Gentle spin

A brief spin in a centrifuge separates the blood so its healing components are concentrated.

3

Careful placement

Katherine places the PRF in the under-eye area with care, based on your plan.

Common questions

Questions about PRF under eye repair

What does PRF under eye repair cost?

Starting prices are $250 for PRF under eye repair. Katherine confirms the exact price for your plan during your free consultation.

How soon will I see results?

Results develop gradually over several weeks. Katherine reviews your progress at follow-up.

What should I expect afterward?

Mild redness, tenderness, or small bumps may appear and usually settle within a few days. Katherine reviews written aftercare with you before you leave.

Is there downtime?

Many services fit into a regular schedule with minimal downtime. Katherine reviews your aftercare steps during your visit.

Who should have a health review first?

Katherine reviews each health history before treatment and may recommend a different option or a short delay when any of the following apply: blood-clotting or platelet disorders, anemia, blood thinners or medications that affect clotting, active infection or skin conditions at the treatment site, pregnancy, breastfeeding, or plans to become pregnant, or active cancer treatment.
